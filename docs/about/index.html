About the project

Why listen first?

This website asks a simple question: what do we miss when we decide who someone is before hearing their story?

In Punching the Air, Amal is a teenager who writes and makes art. But after he is accused of a crime, other people see a label before they see him. His story made us think about how quickly race and assumptions can shape the way a person is judged.

Our project uses a comic strip, the book's poems, and research from justice organizations to help readers slow down. The comic gives a visual starting point. The other pages explain the issue and show where people can keep learning.

Essential question

How can storytelling expose injustice and inspire change?

What we hope visitors take away

A person's life cannot be explained by one accusation or one stereotype. Listening to stories matters, and so does checking the facts behind a claim. This project is a starting point for both.

How to explore

1. Read the comic

Start with the comic cover, then flip to the strip. Use the panel guide if the small text is hard to read.

2. Connect it to the book

See how “Blind Justice,” “Gray Suit,” and “Black Ink” shaped the project.

3. Learn from organizations doing the work

Visit the Equal Justice Initiative, Innocence Project, and Georgia Innocence Project through links on the Issue Overview page.
