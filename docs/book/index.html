Connection to Punching the Air

Amal writes back.

Three poems helped us turn a literary theme into a comic and a website about how people are labeled.

Punching the Air, by Ibi Zoboi and Yusef Salaam, tells Amal's story through poems. His writing gives readers a view of him that a courtroom label cannot provide. These are the three poems named in our planning presentation.

01
Page 44 in our planning notes

“Blind Justice”

Amal questions the idea that everyone has the same choices. The poem connects to our comic because the character looks at a path that seems closed before he has even begun. Its image of “blind Lady Justice” made us ask whether the system is equally fair to everyone.

02
Page 9 in our planning notes

“Gray Suit”

Amal wears a gray suit because people think it will change how he is seen in court. But, as he says, it “didn't make me any less black.” This poem made us think about how appearance is sometimes treated as more important than who a person actually is.

03
Page 20 in our planning notes

“Black Ink”

The contrast “I am ink, he is paper” shows how differently Amal and a white boy can be described. In our project, labels are not just words. They can guide what other people expect to see. That is why the website asks readers to question the first story they hear.

From poem to picture

The comic takes the question in “Blind Justice” and turns it into a sequence of images. The website then connects that fictional question to research about real-world bias and wrongful convictions.
